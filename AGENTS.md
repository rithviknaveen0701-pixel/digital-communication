## Study-note guidelines

1. Follow the **24EC504 syllabus** as the source of truth for topic coverage.
2. Prefer clear explanations aimed at an engineering student preparing for CIE/SEE examinations.
3. Include definitions, intuition, important equations, derivations, assumptions, worked examples, diagrams, and exam-oriented points where appropriate.
4. Clearly distinguish formulas, concepts, derivations, numerical problems, and practical/lab material.
5. Use `[[Wiki Links]]` to connect related notes when the target note is expected to exist in the vault.
6. Use frontmatter consistently for metadata such as module, topic, course code, and status when creating study notes.
7. Use `$$...$$` for display mathematics and `$...$` for inline mathematics where appropriate.
8. Do not silently change syllabus wording or invent syllabus requirements. If adding supplementary material, label it as supplementary.
9. Keep filenames descriptive and Obsidian-friendly.
10. When asked to help study a topic, use the syllabus to identify where the topic belongs and relate it to the relevant course outcome when useful.

## Syllabus modules

- **Module 1:** Introduction to Digital Communication; sources and signals; signal-processing operations; channels; sampling; reconstruction; sampling distortion; practical sampling and signal recovery; TDM (T1 carrier).
- **Module 2:** PCM; quantization noise and SNR; robust quantization; DPCM; DM; baseband transmission and shaping; discrete PAM; PAM power spectra; ISI; Nyquist criterion; correlative coding.
- **Module 3:** Digital modulation; modulation formats; coherent binary modulation; coherent quadrature modulation; non-coherent binary modulation; binary vs. quaternary modulation.
- **Module 4:** Spread-spectrum modulation; PN sequences; spread-spectrum concepts; direct-sequence spread coherent binary PSK; signal-space dimensionality; processing gain; frequency-hopping spread spectrum.

## Practical/lab coverage

The syllabus includes hardware and simulation work involving sampling theorem verification, ASK, FSK, PSK, DPSK, QPSK, PCM, DPCM, DM, and ADM. Keep lab notes separate from theory notes when appropriate.
